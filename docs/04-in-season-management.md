# In-season management

A draft buys you a starting position, not a title. Most leagues are won by the
manager who handles the next fourteen weeks better than everyone else.

## The waiver budget plan

If you run FAAB, treat the season as three phases and budget accordingly.

| Phase | Weeks | Share of budget | What you are buying |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | 1–4 | 35% | The genuine breakouts. Prices are lowest and the remaining season is longest. |
| Consolidation | 5–11 | 40% | Injury replacements and the backs who inherited a job. |
| Championship | 12–17 | 25% | Targeted playoff-schedule plays and one big swing. |

Two rules that matter more than any individual bid:

1. **Never spend more than 35% of your remaining budget on a player who is not a
   week-one starter for you.** Depth you do not start is the most common way
   managers waste a season's budget.
2. **Bid on the role, not the game.** A back who ran 22 times because the starter
   left in the first quarter is worth more than a receiver who caught two long
   touchdowns on three targets.

## Situations to watch from Week 1

These are the specific dominoes this year's research identified. When one falls,
the pickup is already obvious — which means you should be first.

| If this happens | Immediately acquire | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Josh Jacobs stays on the exempt list past Week 3 | MarShawn Lloyd, Kaleb Johnson | Green Bay traded for Johnson specifically as insurance |
| Christian McCaffrey misses any time | Jordan James | The single highest-leverage backup in football |
| Saquon Barkley misses time | Tank Bigsby | First in yards after contact, second in missed tackle rate, fourth in explosive run rate last year |
| Chuba Hubbard's hamstring lingers | Jonathon Brooks | Already trending toward the lead role |
| Kyren Williams stumbles | Blake Corum | Standalone flex value even before a promotion |
| Breece Hall's groin recurs | Braelon Allen | Direct handcuff with goal-line work |
| Atlanta names Penix and he struggles | Fade the whole Atlanta passing game | Drake London's value is quarterback-dependent |
| George Kittle's Achilles setback | Any startable TE | Kittle is the one elite TE with real availability risk |
| Zach Charbonnet is cleared in Seattle | Sell Jadarian Price high | Charbonnet complicates that backfield the moment he is healthy |

## Streaming defenses and kickers

Do not roster two defenses. Stream the unit facing the most turnover-prone
offense each week and spend the roster spot on an upside skill player.

Week 1 specifically: the **Chargers** host Arizona, then get Las Vegas in Week 2.
That is a two-week stream out of the last round of your draft. **Houston** is the
top-projected unit if you would rather set and forget.

Kickers: **Brandon Aubrey** is the one kicker genuinely worth a roster spot ahead
of the last round, because his range makes him a different scoring distribution
rather than a slightly better one. Otherwise take whichever kicker is on a good
offense in a dome and move on.

## Trade posture

The model's fade list is your trade-away list, and the timing matters:

- **Sell the name, buy the role.** Managers overpay for last year's finish.
  De'Von Achane, Bucky Irving and Saquon Barkley all carry more name equity than
  the model thinks the situation supports.
- **Buy injured stars in Weeks 2–4.** Malik Nabers, Cam Skattebo and Tucker Kraft
  are the profile: real talent, a slow ramp, and an owner who drafted them
  expecting immediate production and is now 1–2.
- **Sell handcuffs into news.** The week a starter goes down, his backup is worth
  more than he will ever be again. That is the moment to trade him, not to
  celebrate.

## Bye-week navigation

Week 11 has six teams off — Falcons, Browns, Packers, Rams, Patriots, Seahawks.
Plan for it in Week 9, not Week 11, because everyone else will be bidding on the
same replacements the week it hits.

Week 14 (Cardinals, Cowboys) is the last week before most playoffs and often
decides seeding. Check it before your final roster moves.
