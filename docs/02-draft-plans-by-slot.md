# Draft plans by slot

12-team, full PPR, 1QB / 2RB / 3WR / 1TE / 1FLEX / 1K / 1DST, 16 rounds.

Every plan below is a **priority order, not a script.** The simulator's clearest
finding is that rigid plans lose to flexible ones, because a script forces picks
the board is not offering. Take the highest-value player your plan permits; if
your top two targets are gone, move to the next name rather than reaching.

## The four rules that apply at every slot

1. **Do not take a quarterback before round 8.** Replacement-level QB projects
   around 271 points. Herbert, Dart, Burrow and Murray all rank 25 or more picks
   ahead of their ADP. Every pick spent on a quarterback in rounds 1–7 is a pick
   spent on the smallest positional edge on the board.

   *The one exception, and the model does take it.* Across simulated drafts the
   model's median quarterback comes in round 7 or later, but in roughly one draft
   in ten it takes one in round 3 or 4. That happens when an elite arm — Maye,
   Daniels, Lamar — has slid well past his ADP to a turn while the receiver tier
   in front of you has already broken. If both of those are true, take him. If
   only one is, wait.
2. **Take the last player in a tier, not the first player in the next one.**
   Check `output/tiers.md`. The cliffs are where value lives.
3. **The RB pocket is rounds 5–8.** D'Andre Swift (+23 value over
   replacement), Tony Pollard (+20) and David Montgomery (+15) all clear
   replacement level while costing a fraction of a round-2 back. Note the model
   still prefers receivers at those same picks on raw value — take the backs when
   the receiver tier in front of you has broken, which is what its own drafts do.
4. **Spend your last five picks on ceiling, not floor.** A bench player is worth
   nothing unless he becomes a starter. Handcuff your own backs first, then take
   swings.

## Picks 1–4: you get an anchor, so take one

You have access to a genuinely scarce asset. Take it and do not overthink.

| Slot | Round 1 | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Jahmyr Gibbs** — the only Tier 1 running back on the board | Long wait to 24/25 |
| 2 | **Bijan Robinson** | 23/26 turn |
| 3 | **Ja'Marr Chase** — best receiver, and the RB3 tier is a cliff below Bijan | 22/27 |
| 4 | **Puka Nacua** or **Jaxon Smith-Njigba** | 21/28 |

Gibbs is the model's clear 1.01: David Montgomery was traded to Houston, and
Gibbs was already at 14.4 carries and 5.6 receptions per game on ~75% of snaps in
the second half of 2025. He is a tier of one.

**At your round 2/3 turn** the elite tight ends (ADP 17–18) will be gone. Your
targets, in order: **Nico Collins** (Jayden Higgins' ACL removes his only
competition), **George Pickens** (WR6 finish last year, drafted at WR12),
**Omarion Hampton**, **Rashee Rice**, **Malik Nabers** if you can absorb the ACL
ramp-up.

**Rounds 4–6:** take **Tyler Warren** or **Kyle Pitts** at tight end — Pitts is
the value, because Kevin Stefanski has produced a top-12 tight end four years
running. Then attack the running back pocket.

**From pick 1 specifically:** you are picking 1, 24, 25, 48, 49, 72, 73. Those
back-to-back turns are an advantage — you can take two players from the same
tier before it breaks. Plan them in pairs.

## Picks 5–8: the balanced middle, and the best tight-end access

This is where the elite tight ends actually reach you, and it is the single
biggest structural edge available in this draft.

| Slot | Round 1 target | Round 2 target |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Jaxon Smith-Njigba / Christian McCaffrey | Brock Bowers or Trey McBride |
| 6 | Christian McCaffrey / Amon-Ra St. Brown | Brock Bowers or Trey McBride |
| 7 | Amon-Ra St. Brown / Jonathan Taylor | Bowers, McBride, or A.J. Brown |
| 8 | Jonathan Taylor / James Cook | Bowers or McBride (they often reach here) |

**Take the tight end.** Bowers and McBride project around 250 points against a
tight-end replacement level near 119. That is a bigger positional edge than any
running back available in round 2, and it is the one advantage that holds every
single week rather than needing a matchup.

Christian McCaffrey is a real option in the middle of round 1, but he is
projected for 14.5 games, not 17. If you take him, **you must handcuff him with
Jordan James** — the highest-leverage backup in football — around round 10.

**Rounds 3–5:** receivers. Emeka Egbuka, Ladd McConkey, Zay Flowers and Tetairoa
McMillan are the model's favourite mid-round targets.

**Rounds 5–8:** the running back pocket, then your quarterback around round 8–9.

## Picks 9–12: back-to-back turns are a weapon

You miss the elite tier, but you pick twice in a row nine times. Use it to take
tier-enders in pairs.

| Slot | Round 1/2 turn | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Cook / Chase Brown, then Bowers or McBride | The TEs frequently last to 16 |
| 10 | Achane / Chase Brown, then McBride or A.J. Brown | Fade Achane — see below |
| 11 | Jefferson / Chase Brown, then Bowers / McBride / Jeanty | |
| 12 | **Ja'Marr Chase or Justin Jefferson**, then CeeDee Lamb / Derrick Henry | Two picks, 13 seconds apart |

**Pick 12 is not a bad slot this year.** Chase or Jefferson at 12 and then Lamb
or Henry at 13 is a stronger start than picks 8–10 typically produce, because the
running backs between 8 and 15 are exactly the ones the model fades.

**The one trap at 10–12:** De'Von Achane at ADP 10. Mike McDaniel is gone, Malik
Willis is the quarterback, and the new coordinator is expected to run more. He is
the loudest fade on the board and he will be sitting there looking like value.

## The rounds where drafts are actually won

**Rounds 5–8 — the running back pocket.** Every year the position everyone
panicked about in round 2 is available at a fraction of the cost four rounds
later. The three the model actually likes here are **D'Andre Swift** (ADP 55,
+23 VOR), **Tony Pollard** (52, +20) and **David Montgomery** (58, +15).

Two names commonly recommended in this range that the model does *not* endorse
at cost: RJ Harvey (63) is stuck in a three-back Payton committee, and Bhayshul
Tuten (78) has the clear runway everyone likes but only projects a point above
replacement. Take them as upside darts two rounds later, not as your RB2.

**Rounds 8–10 — quarterback.** Justin Herbert at 92 with Mike McDaniel calling
plays and Keenan Allen gone to Indianapolis. Jaxson Dart at 100, who was QB3 in
fantasy points per dropback in 2025 behind only Allen and Maye. Joe Burrow at 60
if you want the safer version. Take one. Do not take two before round 12.

**Rounds 10–12 — the tight end you skipped.** If you did not take Bowers or
McBride, this is where the plan pays off: Jake Ferguson at 107 (TE8 in two of the
last three seasons) and Chig Okonkwo at 115 (open targets while defences key on
McLaurin and Diggs).

**Rounds 11–14 — handcuffs and swings.** Handcuff your own backs first: Jordan
James behind McCaffrey, Tank Bigsby behind Barkley, Blake Corum behind Kyren
Williams, Braelon Allen behind Hall. Then swing on Tre' Harris, De'Zhaun
Stribling, Michael Wilson and Xavier Worthy.

**Rounds 15–16 — kicker and defence, in that order, and not before.** Brandon
Aubrey is the one kicker whose range genuinely changes his scoring distribution.
The Chargers defence hosts Arizona in Week 1 and Las Vegas in Week 2 — a
two-week stream out of your last pick.

## A note on the tight-end fork

There are two coherent plans and no third one:

1. **Pay for Bowers or McBride in round 2.** You get roughly 130 points of
   positional edge that shows up every week. Best available from picks 5–11.
2. **Wait entirely and take Ferguson or Okonkwo in round 11.** You spend the
   saved capital on receivers in rounds 2–4.

What loses is the middle: paying rounds 5–7 for the third or fourth tight end.
Colston Loveland and Tyler Warren are good players and genuine values at their
ADP — but if you are taking one, take him as the *plan*, not as a consolation
after missing the top two.
