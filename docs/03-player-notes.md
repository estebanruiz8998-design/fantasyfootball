**Jayden Higgins (WR, HOU) tore his ACL and is out for the season.** That is the
single cleanest positive signal in the research: it concentrates targets on Nico
Collins, who the model already liked.

**Josh Jacobs (RB, GB) is on the Commissioner Exempt List** after a domestic
abuse arrest, charged with two misdemeanours. He is paid but cannot practise,
and his first court date is in November — Week 11. Green Bay traded for Kaleb
Johnson from Pittsburgh, which tells you what the team expects. Do not draft
Jacobs in redraft. The beneficiaries are MarShawn Lloyd (surged 56 spots to
RB33) and Johnson, though Lloyd has played one game in two years.

## Offseason moves that reshaped the board

- **David Montgomery traded DET → HOU.** Jahmyr Gibbs is now a true bellcow. In
  the second half of 2025 he was already at 14.4 carries and 5.6 receptions per
  game on ~75% of snaps. This is the No. 1 overall pick.
- **D.J. Moore traded CHI → BUF.** He leads Buffalo in targets, but Josh Allen
  averaged 28.75 pass attempts per game last season. The target share is real;
  the volume behind it is not.
- **Jaylen Waddle traded MIA → DEN.** A 1a/1b with Courtland Sutton for Bo Nix.
  This is why Sutton is a fade.
- **Kenneth Walker III → KC.** The best situation of his career. He has never
  finished above RB18; Kansas City's scoring environment is how that changes.
- **Kyler Murray → MIN**, **Tua Tagovailoa → ATL**, **Travis Etienne → NO**,
  **Keenan Allen → IND**, **Stefon Diggs → WAS**, **Kenneth Gainwell → TB**.
- **Kevin Stefanski is the Falcons head coach.** He has produced a top-12 tight
  end in each of the past four seasons, which is the entire Kyle Pitts thesis.
- **Mike McDaniel is the Chargers offensive coordinator.** With Keenan Allen
  gone, this is the Ladd McConkey and Justin Herbert thesis.
- **Klint Kubiak to the Raiders** with No. 1 overall pick Fernando Mendoza.
  Helps Ashton Jeanty and Brock Bowers.
- **Zac Robinson to Tampa Bay.** Emeka Egbuka is the clear alpha in a
  McVay-derived scheme.
- **Arizona took Jeremiyah Love No. 3 overall.** The Cardinals threw at the
  highest rate in the NFL in 2025; that is not repeating. It caps Marvin
  Harrison Jr. and trims Trey McBride's volume — though McBride still went
  126/1,239/11 and Jacoby Brissett was always the quarterback who fed him.

## An important caveat on the "reaches" list

The model's biggest apparent reaches are almost all late-round running backs —
Chuba Hubbard, MarShawn Lloyd, Jordan James, Zach Charbonnet, Braelon Allen. Do
not read that as "never draft these players."

A season-long point projection cannot express **contingent value**. Jordan James
projects for 102 points as Christian McCaffrey's backup, and that number is
correct — but it is the average of "McCaffrey plays 16 games and James scores 60"
and "McCaffrey misses eight games and James finishes as a low-end RB1." The
draft simulator captures this correctly, because it models injuries and gives a
handcuff bonus when you already own the starter. The static cheat sheet does not.

Read the cheat sheet's ADP-edge column for **starters**, and trust the simulator
for **backups**.

| 13 | Ravens, Colts, Raiders, Jets |
| 14 | Cardinals, Cowboys |

**Week 11 is the problem** — six teams are off. If you finish a draft with Puka
Nacua, Drake London, Kyle Pitts, Tucker Kraft and Jaxon Smith-Njigba, you have
built a bye-week disaster. The model applies a penalty for stacking same-position
starters on one bye, but check Week 11 manually before you submit your last
picks.

Week 14 matters too: it is the last week before most fantasy playoffs, and both
Arizona and Dallas are off. If you own Trey McBride, Jeremiyah Love, CeeDee Lamb
and George Pickens, that is a Week 14 hole in a week that often decides seeding.
