Method, and what it cannot do

How the numbers are made

Positional scoring curves are anchored to published 2026 projections and interpolated in log-rank space, then scaled by expected games played and a research-driven per-game adjustment. The most useful anchor is a published George Pickens line — 82.6 receptions, 1,201 yards, 8.4 touchdowns — because it can be verified: that is 253 PPR points against a quoted 252.

Value over replacement uses actual starter demand, with flex slots awarded one at a time to whichever of RB, WR or TE wins them. Then the whole draft is simulated, because a cheat sheet cannot tell you whether a plan survives eleven other drafters.

Four bugs that changed the answer

  • The board ran out. A 12-team, 16-round draft consumes 192 picks; the pool held fewer. Drafts silently truncated and thin rosters looked survivable.
  • Raw VOR stacked eight receivers. A sixth receiver has a large value over replacement and almost no marginal value, because he cannot get into the lineup.
  • Marginal value drafted a quarterback first overall. With an empty roster, absolute points win. Adding opportunity cost — value over next available — is what moved this strategy from worst to best.
  • Opportunity cost went degenerate at turns. At a snake turn your picks are adjacent (48, then 49), so every position’s next-available player is the same one you would take anyway and every comparison collapses to zero. The horizon now skips adjacent picks. Worth 30–45 points a season.

What this cannot do

  • Projections are reconstructed, not scraped. Direct page fetches were blocked in the build environment, so figures come from search-result summaries. Curve shapes are anchored to published numbers, but individual players inherit their positional rank from consensus. A player ranked wrong is projected wrong.
  • ADP is estimated for most players. Directly sourced values are used where known — Barkley 13, Walker 22.3, Hampton 25.3, Garrett Wilson 38 — and the rest are interpolated. Check your own platform: ADP varies enormously between Sleeper, ESPN and NFFC.
  • News moves faster than this page. Everything is current to 4 September. Verify Nabers, Kittle, Kraft, Skattebo and the Atlanta quarterback job before you draft.
  • Simulation differences under ~25 points are noise. Read the strategy chart as an ordering, not a measurement.

If your league is different

Superflex changes everything — take two quarterbacks in the first four rounds. TE premium pushes Bowers and McBride into round one outright. Half PPR moves Derrick Henry and Mike Evans up, Wan’Dale Robinson and Josh Downs down. In best ball, ceiling beats floor and handcuffs are nearly worthless.
